A member rarely cancels overnight. Before they fail to renew, they almost always go through a few weeks of coming less often: from four times a week down to two, then one, then nothing. If you watch attendance closely, that warning arrives with enough time to do something about it.

This guide explains what to look at, how to run the numbers in a spreadsheet and what to say to a member when they show up on the list. You do not need special software to start, only a log of check-ins.

## Compare each member with themselves

A general rule such as "alert me if someone comes less than twice a week" does not work. Some members have always come once a week and are happy that way. Look at the change instead: someone who used to come four times and now comes twice is telling you something, even though twice a week is a good number.

A simple way to do it is to compare the last three weeks with the six weeks before them. If a member's frequency has dropped to half or less, they deserve a message.

The six reference weeks keep one bad week (a trip, a cold, a busy week at work) from triggering an alert. Three recent weeks are enough to see a trend without waiting until the member is already gone.

## How to calculate it in a spreadsheet

If your front desk logs check-ins, you can build the list once a month in Excel or Google Sheets:

1. Export or copy the check-ins from the last nine weeks with three columns: member, date and time.
2. Add a column with the week each check-in belongs to (the `WEEKNUM` function works).
3. Use a pivot table to count how many check-ins each member had per week.
4. Work out two averages per member: weeks 1 to 6 and weeks 7 to 9.
5. Divide the recent average by the reference average. Members with a result of 0.5 or lower go on your list.

Leave out members who joined less than six weeks ago, since they do not have a reference of their own yet. Track them separately, as described below.

## What to do when you spot one

Have a person write to them. A short message from a trainer who knows them works better than an automated promotion:

> Hi Camila, we haven't seen you at functional these past few weeks. Everything OK? If the 7 a.m. slot no longer works for you, we're opening one at 7:30 p.m. on Thursday.

In Colombia that message almost always goes over WhatsApp. Send it from the gym's number, with the trainer's name, and without attaching offers. The point is to ask, not to sell.

The reason is often practical, and each one calls for a different answer:

- If their work or class schedule changed, show them the classes or time slots that could fit now.
- If they got injured, offering to freeze the plan is usually better than losing the member. When they come back, a return plan with a trainer helps them stick with it.
- If cost is the issue and you have a session pack or a shorter plan, it is better for them to switch than to leave.
- If they lost motivation, a group class or a short challenge with other members tends to work better than a discount.

Note what they told you on the member's record. Over time you will see which reasons repeat and which ones depend on you.

## Look at the schedule too

When several members of the same class drop off at the same time, the problem may not be them. Check whether the trainer changed, whether the room is packed at peak hours or whether the class moved to a different time. Sorting your list by class or time slot shows those patterns quickly.

## Pay attention to new members

The first two or three months are when the habit is easiest to lose. A new member does not have a frequency of their own to compare against yet, so the rule is simpler: if they come less than once a week in their first month, write to them. They often do not know which class to take or do not know anyone yet, and a specific invitation solves that.

## Check whether it is working

Keep count of how many members you contacted each month and how many went back to their previous frequency within the next four weeks. You do not need a dashboard; one more column in the same sheet is enough. If almost nobody comes back, review the message and when you send it. If they come back but leave the following month, the underlying reason was not solved.
